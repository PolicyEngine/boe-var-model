### Read
- GDP: agreement throughout. Every BoE point sits inside the SVAR 68% band, gaps <= 0.4pp.
  The SVAR misses BoE's 2028 pickup (BoE has growth accelerating as the real-income drag fades;
  the SVAR flattens at ~1.45%).
- CPI near term: 2.61 vs 2.9 in 2026Q3. BoE has more data and forecasts higher: the July MPR puts
  a +0.6pp direct energy contribution into 2026Q3 CPI and a real-income drag through 2027. Our
  origin is 2026Q1 data, so the SVAR largely predates that shock - which also explains why BoE
  GDP runs 0.3pp BELOW ours in 2026-27.
- CPI medium term: the real divergence. BoE returns near target (1.8-1.9% by 2028-29); the SVAR
  sits flat at ~2.95%, ~1.1pp above, landing on BoE's adverse scenario (2.8% in 2028Q3).
- Mechanism, checked: the SVAR's median Bank Rate path barely moves - 3.74% in 2026Q2 rising to
  3.91% by 2029Q3, i.e. roughly the current 3.75% held flat - against BoE's conditioning curve of
  3.8% then 4.2% from 2027. So the SVAR delivers ~30-40bp LESS tightening than the market curve
  BoE conditions on, and has no target-anchoring mechanism beyond its estimated rate rule. Note
  the ~2.95% CPI plateau is NOT the sample mean (1993Q1-2025Q1 mean YoY CPI is 2.38%, GDP 1.93%);
  it is the estimated unconditional mean of the full-sample VAR, raised by the 2021-23 episode
  sitting in the data the constant is fitted to (the Covid dummies stop at 2021Q2).
- Bands: the 68% CPI band is ~2.6pp wide by 2027 and spans BoE's central, milder and adverse
  paths - at this horizon the SVAR does not discriminate between them.

## Part 2: what causes the gap, and the OBR comparison

## 2.1 The OBR has no inflation equation to compare against

Source: the OBR's published 372-equation model, October 2025 vintage, as carried in the
PolicyEngine `obr-macroeconomic-model` repo at
`obr_macro/_data/obr_model_code_october_2025.txt`. Line numbers are that file's.

- Line 314: `'CPI = CPI(-1) * (CPIX^(1-W1) * CPIRENT^W1) / ...` - leading apostrophe;
  in EViews that is a COMMENT. The CPI equation is switched off.
- Line 284, under the annotation `'re-written to enable indirect T2 fix:`
  `dlog(MKR) = (dlog(CPI) - W1*dlog(CPIRENT) - (1-W1)*dlog(RPCOST)) / (1-W1)`
  i.e. the identity is inverted and solved for the retail mark-up MKR. CPI is the
  INPUT; the mark-up is the residual that absorbs whatever CPI is assumed to be.
- CPI then flows downstream only: into wages (line 232, `dlog(PSAVEI)` has a
  `+0.282*(dlog(CPI)-dlog(PMSGVA))` real-wage term) and into the consumption
  deflator (line 333, `PCE/PCE(-4) = CPI/CPI(-4)`). Nothing feeds back into CPI.
- The emulator's own scorecard agrees: `docs/calibration_scorecard.md` line 47 lists
  "CPI, CPI inflation, GDP deflator" as **passthrough (held at OBR value)**, 0.00% error
  by construction.

So there is no Phillips curve and no output-gap-to-inflation channel in the OBR model.
Its inflation forecast is a judgement path imposed exogenously. The comparison to make
is against that imposed path, not against an equation.

## 2.3 What actually causes the SVAR's ~1pp CPI gap - decomposed

Two candidate mechanisms, both tested rather than asserted.

**(a) Rate path: small. ~0.06pp.**
The SVAR's own median Bank Rate goes 3.74% (2026Q2) -> 3.91% (2029Q3), i.e. broadly
today's 3.75% held flat, against BoE's conditioning curve of 3.8% then 4.2%. That is a
~35bp shortfall. The identified UK monetary policy shock moves Bank Rate 0.106pp on
impact and lowers YoY CPI by 0.018-0.024pp at h=8-13. Scaled to 35bp, the missing
tightening explains **0.06-0.08pp** of inflation at the 2028-29 horizon. This is a
partial-equilibrium scaling of the identified MP-shock IRF and ignores expectations /
anchoring effects the SVAR cannot represent - but on its own terms, essentially none
of the gap.

**(b) Estimation window: this is what sets the long-run level. ~0.95pp.**
Re-estimating on 1992Q1-2019Q4 (pre-Covid, so the 2021-23 inflation episode is out of
the parameters) and forecasting from the same 2026Q1 origin:

| Quarter | SVAR full (1992-2025Q1) | SVAR pre-Covid (1992-2019Q4) | BoE | OBR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026Q3 | 2.61 | 2.19 | 2.9 | 2.08 |
| 2027Q3 | 2.96 | 1.97 | 2.6 | 2.01 |
| 2028Q3 | 2.93 | 2.00 | 1.8 | 2.02 |
| 2029Q3 | 2.97 | 1.99 | 1.9 | 2.00 |

The pre-Covid SVAR lands on 2.0% - on top of the OBR and within 0.1-0.2pp of BoE, with
no anchor imposed and no conditioning path.

Read this carefully: it is NOT a controlled "spike in / spike out" test. The pre-Covid
VAR's median Bank Rate falls to **1.81%** by 2029Q3 - the 2010s low-rate regime, ~240bp
BELOW BoE's 4.2% conditioning curve. It delivers 2.0% inflation despite far looser policy
than either comparator. So the right reading is not "the spike raises inflation holding
policy fixed"; it is that **the VAR's entire long-run equilibrium - rates, inflation,
growth together - is whatever its estimation window says.** With 1992-2019 that
equilibrium is 2% inflation at ~1.8% Bank Rate; with 2021-23 added it is ~3% inflation at
~3.9% Bank Rate. Neither is anchored to anything; both are sample artefacts.

Note the ~2.95% plateau is also not the historical sample mean (mean YoY CPI 1993Q1-2025Q1
is 2.38%, GDP 1.93%). It is the estimated unconditional mean of the full-sample VAR, which
the 2021-23 episode raises through the estimated constant - the Covid dummies cover only
2020Q1-2021Q2, so the 2021-23 inflation run is left in the data the constant is fitted to.

## 2.4 The one-line answer

The gap is not a monetary-policy-path disagreement and not a data-vintage artefact. It
is that BoE and OBR both impose a 2% anchor - OBR literally, as an exogenous CPI path
that converges to 2.000 because the model's CPI equation is commented out; BoE through
MPC judgement - while the SVAR has no anchor at all. Estimated on 1992-2025Q1 it settles
at ~2.95% (with Bank Rate ~3.9%); estimated on 1992-2019Q4 it settles at 2.0% (with Bank
Rate ~1.8%). The model is reporting its estimation window, not an inflation forecast.

GDP is a different story: all three agree (SVAR 1.4-1.5%, OBR 1.5-1.6%, BoE 1.1-1.7%),
because trend growth is far less sensitive to which sample you estimate on.
